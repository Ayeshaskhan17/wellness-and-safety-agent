SECTION 1: MINDFULNESS, BREATHING, AND GROUNDING EXERCISES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

* The 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding Technique (For Acute Anxiety & Panic)
  When feeling overwhelmed, anxious, or detached, focus on your surroundings and identify:
  - 5 things you can see (e.g., a chair, a plant, a spot on the wall).
  - 4 things you can physically feel (e.g., your feet on the floor, the texture of your shirt).
  - 3 things you can hear (e.g., traffic outside, a fan hum, birds chirping).
  - 2 things you can smell (e.g., soap, coffee, fresh air).
  - 1 thing you can taste (e.g., toothpaste, mint, water).
  This shifts the brain's focus away from internal anxious thoughts and back to the physical present.

* 4-7-8 Deep Breathing Exercise (For Immediate Calming and Sleep)
  1. Empty the lungs of air.
  2. Breathe in quietly through the nose for 4 seconds.
  3. Hold your breath gently for a count of 7 seconds.
  4. Exhale completely through your mouth, making a "whoosh" sound, for 8 seconds.
  5. Repeat this cycle up to 4 times. 
  Note: This naturally stimulates the parasympathetic nervous system, lowering heart rate and blood pressure.

* Box Breathing Technique (Used by High-Stress Professionals)
  1. Inhale slowly through your nose for a count of 4.
  2. Hold your breath for a count of 4.
  3. Exhale slowly through your mouth for a count of 4.
  4. Hold your lungs empty for a count of 4.
  5. Repeat for 3 to 5 cycles to restore mental clarity.

SECTION 2: COPING STRATEGIES FOR BURNOUT & DAILY ACADEMIC STRESS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Managing Academic and Career Burnout
  Burnout is a state of emotional, physical, and mental exhaustion caused by excessive and prolonged stress.
  - The Rule of "Micro-Breaks": For every 45 minutes of intense mental focus, take a mandatory 5-minute break. Step away from all screens, stretch, or drink water.
  - Task Chunking: Break large, intimidating projects or assignments into micro-tasks that take less than 15 minutes to complete. This lowers the psychological barrier to starting.
  - The Power of Saying No: Protect your mental bandwidth. Assess requests before agreeing. If a task compromises your sleep or basic wellness, politely decline or renegotiate the deadline.

* Cognitive Reframing for Imposter Syndrome
  - Imposter syndrome makes individuals feel like "frauds" despite their hard work.
  - Reframing: Replace the thought "I don't belong here" with "I am here to learn, and it is entirely okay not to know everything right now." Focus on your personal growth journey rather than comparing yourself to others.

SECTION 3: SAFE JOURNALING & EMOTIONAL EXPRESSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Safe Journaling Guidelines
  Writing down your thoughts can dramatically reduce cognitive overload. Use these prompts to safely process difficult feelings:
  - Prompt 1: "What is the single heaviest thought on my mind right now, and is it within my control to change it today?"
  - Prompt 2: "What are three small boundaries I can set tomorrow to protect my peace of mind?"
  - Prompt 3: "Write down exactly what you are feeling without trying to edit, judge, or fix it."

SECTION 4: IDENTIFYING DISTRESS SIGNALS (EARLY DETECTION)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Common Distress Signs to Monitor:
  - Emotional Symptoms: Persistent feelings of hopelessness, severe guilt, numbness, or overwhelming dread.
  - Cognitive Symptoms: Difficulty concentrating, memory lapses, constant racing thoughts, or highly self-critical inner dialogue.
  - Behavioral Changes: Social withdrawal, neglecting personal hygiene, severe changes in sleep patterns (insomnia or oversleeping), or sudden irritability.
  - High-Risk Indicators: Expressing feelings of being a burden to others, talking about having "no way out," or expressing direct thoughts of self-harm.
